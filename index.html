01 — Introduction

An RC car (remote-controlled car) is a vehicle that is operated from a distance using a handheld transmitter. Our model uses four powered wheels, so it is a 4WD vehicle.

What does 4WD mean?All four wheels receive driving power from the motors. This can improve traction on suitable surfaces.
What is remote control?A transmitter sends commands to a receiver installed in the car.
Project goalBuild a controllable model that can move forward, reverse, turn left and turn right.

Where are RC cars useful?

They are used for learning robotics, exploring radio control, testing vehicle designs, recreation and demonstrating basic engineering concepts.

The car is a small educational model. Its speed, traction and running time depend on the motors, battery, tyres, gearing, surface and total weight.

Each component has a specific job. The mechanical parts make the vehicle move, while the electronic parts receive commands and control motor power.

Movement commands

  • Forward: left and right wheels rotate to drive the car ahead.
  • Reverse: motor direction is reversed.
  • Left turn: the left and right sides are driven at different speeds or in opposite directions.
  • Right turn: the speed difference is reversed.
  • Stop: the motor drivers stop supplying drive power.

The exact receiver output type and control interface must match the motor-control circuit. A receiver output should not be connected directly to a motor.

04 — Motor and Drive System

A DC gear motor combines an electric motor with a gearbox. The gearbox reduces speed and increases available turning force compared with the motor shaft alone.

Why use four motors?

  • Each wheel can have its own drive motor.
  • Power can be arranged as a left-side pair and a right-side pair.
  • Electronic control of the two sides allows skid steering.

Left/right drive concept

Left sideFront-left motor + rear-left motor are controlled together.
Right sideFront-right motor + rear-right motor are controlled together.
Before connecting two motors in parallel to one driver, check that the combined running current and starting/stall current stay within the driver's and wiring's safe limits. Motors on the same side should have matching specifications.

Wheel direction

Because motors may be mounted as mirror images, the polarity needed for forward movement may differ between sides. Test with the wheels lifted safely and correct the wiring or control direction as required.

05 — Battery and Power Supply

The battery supplies the energy needed by the motors and control electronics. Correct voltage, current capability and protection are essential.

Battery checks

  • Confirm the battery's cell count and fully charged voltage—not just its nominal label.
  • Confirm that the motors and motor drivers are suitable for the battery voltage.
  • Use a suitable LiPo balance charger and follow the battery manufacturer's instructions.
  • Use a correctly rated fuse and wiring appropriate for the expected current.
  • Provide a suitable regulated supply for the receiver if its required voltage differs from the battery voltage.
Never connect a LiPo battery directly to an unknown circuit. Do not short its terminals, puncture it, leave it charging unattended or use a swollen, damaged or unusually hot battery. Ask a knowledgeable adult to check the power circuit before testing.

06 — Chassis and Mechanical Design

The chassis is the frame that holds the motors, battery, receiver, motor drivers and other parts. It should be rigid enough to support the vehicle without being unnecessarily heavy.

Base plateSupports the motors and main electronics. Keep it flat and strong.
Motor mountsHold each motor firmly and keep the shafts aligned with the wheel adapters.
WheelsProvide grip. Check that all wheels rotate freely without rubbing the chassis.
Top cover / guardsHelp protect electronics and reduce accidental contact with moving parts.

Good layout practices

  • Place the battery low and secure it firmly.
  • Keep wires away from wheels, shafts and other moving parts.
  • Leave room for airflow around motor drivers.
  • Use clamps and fasteners that cannot loosen during movement.
  • Make the battery and main switch accessible.

07 — Assembly Process

Build in stages. Check each stage before moving to the next one.

  1. Prepare the chassis: mark and make mounting points for the four motors.
  2. Mount the motors: secure all four motors and check alignment.
  3. Fit wheels: attach wheel adapters and make sure each wheel is secure.
  4. Mount electronics: fix the receiver and motor driver modules where they are protected and ventilated.
  5. Secure the battery: use a proper holder or strap so it cannot slide.
  6. Route the wiring: separate signal wires from high-current motor wiring where practical; protect exposed connections.
  7. Inspect: check polarity, loose strands, shorts and mechanical interference before connecting the battery.
  8. Test safely: lift the wheels clear of the floor for the first motor-direction test.
Do not hold the wheels while the motors are powered. Keep hands, hair and loose clothing away from rotating parts.

08 — Control, Wiring and Testing

The receiver's control signals must be translated into suitable motor-driver inputs. The exact wiring depends on the receiver output mode and the control circuit being used.

System-level connection plan

ConnectionWhat to verify
Transmitter ↔ receiverCorrect model binding, compatible frequency/system and working control channels.
Receiver → control electronicsSignal type, channel mapping, signal ground and safe input voltage.
Control electronics → BTS7960Correct logic signals and driver-specific pin requirements.
Battery → driversCorrect polarity, fuse, wire gauge and voltage rating.
Drivers → motorsCorrect motor grouping and direction; current within ratings.

First test checklist

  • Wheels lifted and vehicle secured.
  • Transmitter and receiver bound and switched on in the correct order.
  • Throttle at neutral before enabling motor power.
  • Test one side at a time at low power.
  • Confirm forward, reverse and steering directions.
  • Stop immediately if wiring heats up, a motor stalls or a driver indicates a fault.
This guide is a system overview, not a pin-by-pin wiring diagram. Confirm the exact receiver model, driver board version and motor current before making final connections.

09 — Troubleshooting

Use a step-by-step approach. Disconnect the battery before inspecting or changing wiring.

ProblemPossible checks
Car does not respondCheck transmitter battery, receiver power, binding and channel settings.
Only one side movesInspect that side's driver, motor connections, signal wiring and power path.
Car turns the wrong wayCheck motor orientation, polarity and steering/throttle channel mapping.
Motors stop under loadCheck for jammed wheels, excessive weight, low battery voltage or current limiting.
Driver becomes hotStop testing; check motor stall current, cooling, wiring and driver suitability.
Car moves at neutralRecheck transmitter trims, failsafe settings and control signal calibration.

Keep a test log

Record the battery voltage, surface, test duration, motor/driver temperature observations and any changes made. Change one thing at a time so the cause of a problem is easier to identify.

10 — Applications, Learning and Conclusion

An RC car is more than a moving model. It demonstrates how mechanical engineering, electronics, radio communication and problem-solving work together.

EngineeringChassis design, weight distribution, wheel alignment and traction.
ElectronicsPower supplies, motor drivers, signal wiring and electrical protection.
Programming & controlInterpreting commands, mixing steering and throttle, and failsafe behaviour.
TestingMeasuring performance, finding faults and improving reliability.

Possible future improvements

  • Add headlights and brake lights.
  • Add a low-battery warning.
  • Use a speed controller with suitable protection and telemetry.
  • Explore camera-based driving only with safe, supervised operation.
  • Compare performance on different safe surfaces.
Conclusion: The 4WD RC car shows how a radio transmitter, receiver, motor drivers, battery, motors and chassis combine to create a remotely operated vehicle. Careful component matching, safe wiring and systematic testing are the keys to a reliable build.
